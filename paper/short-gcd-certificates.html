Short remainder-only certificates for the greatest common divisor

October 16, 2026

Abstract

We give a remainder-only straight-line derivation of the greatest common divisor of two positive integers using O(loga/logloga)O(\log a/\log\log a) remainder calls, uniformly for a>b≥1a>b\geq1. Previously generated values may be retained and reused, but numerical constants and other arithmetic operations are not available as instructions. The construction combines a stored Euclidean prefix with three-call contractions and two smaller recursive derivations. It yields the same upper bound for intrinsic remainder-call complexity, and therefore a negative answer to the uniform-process strengthening discussed by Moschovakis. It does not establish a deterministic remainder-only algorithm with this cost. The mathematical proof below and the exact scope of the accompanying Lean verification are distinguished in the final section.

Statement and relation to the book

Throughout, logarithms are to base 22. A derivation starts with its two inputs. An instruction adds rem(x,y)\operatorname{rem}(x,y), where x,yx,y are already available and y>0y>0. Earlier values remain available. No constants are supplied. Let G(a,b)G(a,b) be the minimum number of instructions needed to make gcd(a,b)\gcd(a,b) available, and let D(a,b)=G(a,b)D(a,b)=G(a,b) for coprime inputs. Thus G(a,0)=0G(a,0)=0 and D(a,1)=0D(a,1)=0. These are costs of generating values.

Theorem 1. Uniformly for integers a>b≥1a>b\geq1, G(a,b)=O(logalogloga).G(a,b)=O\left(\frac{\log a}{\log\log a}\right). In particular this bound holds for D(a,b)D(a,b) when gcd(a,b)=1\gcd(a,b)=1.

As usual, the asymptotic assertion concerns sufficiently large aa; a bound valid at every positive input is O(1+log(2+a)/loglog(4+a))O(1+\log(2+a)/\log\log(4+a)).

Moschovakis’s Main Conjecture concerns logarithmic lower bounds for gcd algorithms from rem,eq0\operatorname{rem},\operatorname{eq}_0 [1, p. 2]. The uniform-process strengthening is discussed after Corollary 6C.7 [1, p. 188]; see also (4F-5) on p. 151. Our conclusion addresses that strengthening. The definitions of generated certificates and intrinsic norms are in [1, secs. 1D, 4C–4E]. Theorem 4F.1 [1, pp. 149–150] transfers pointwise intrinsic bounds to a single uniform process. Normalization

Write g=gcd(a,b)>0g=\gcd(a,b)>0. For positive yy, rem(gx,gy)=grem(x,y).\operatorname{rem}(gx,gy)=g\operatorname{rem}(x,y). Every generated value is divisible by gg. Consequently scaling a derivation, or dividing all its values by gg, preserves its length and the validity of every instruction. Hence G(a,b)=D(a/g,b/g).\label{eq:normalize} G(a,b)=D(a/g,b/g). The normalized pair (1,0)(1,0) is understood to have cost zero. Define T(n)=max0≤b≤a≤2n,a>0G(a,b).T(n)=\max_{0\leq b\leq a\leq 2^n,\ a>0}G(a,b). Euclid’s algorithm makes this a finite maximum. Normalization reduces an upper bound for TT to coprime inputs, with equality, zero inputs, and inputs equal to one handled without calls.

Certificates and uniform processes

Use the book’s partial remainder operation: its second argument must be nonzero. Write 𝒩0=(ℕ,rem,eq0)\mathcal N_0=(\mathbb N,\operatorname{rem},\operatorname{eq}_0) and 𝒩ε=(ℕ,rem,eq0,eq1)\mathcal N_\varepsilon=(\mathbb N,\operatorname{rem},\operatorname{eq}_0,\operatorname{eq}_1); compare [1, pp. (1C–10), pp. 24, 142–143]. A derivation determines a finite generated partial substructure by recording its remainder equations. Repeated equations can be discarded.

For the relation gcd(a,b)=1\gcd(a,b)=1 on all natural inputs, a derivation of 11 together with eq1(1)=tt\operatorname{eq}_1(1)=\mathrm{tt} certifies the relation: every common divisor of the images of the inputs divides every transported generated value, hence divides 11. Conversely, without a positive eq1\operatorname{eq}_1 fact the map x↦2xx\mapsto2x preserves every recorded remainder and zero-test fact, as well as every negative one-test fact. It cannot preserve coprimeness. A certificate therefore reaches 11. Enumerating a finite generated diagram in generation order gives a derivation using no more calls than the number of remainder facts. Consequently, on coprime inputs, callsrem(𝒩ε,gcd=1,a,b)=D(a,b),calls(𝒩ε,gcd=1,a,b)=D(a,b)+1.\operatorname{calls}_{\operatorname{rem}}(\mathcal N_\varepsilon,\gcd=1,a,b)=D(a,b),\qquad \operatorname{calls}(\mathcal N_\varepsilon,\gcd=1,a,b)=D(a,b)+1. If the relation is instead defined as a>0∧b>0∧gcd(a,b)=1a>0\wedge b>0\wedge\gcd(a,b)=1, add the two input-positivity facts. The remainder count is unchanged, but one should state the full count as D(a,b)+O(1)D(a,b)+O(1) rather than assert the exact D+1D+1 formula without addressing positivity. For example, the single fact eq1(1)=tt\operatorname{eq}_1(1)=\mathrm{tt} at (2,1)(2,1) allows the image (0,1)(0,1).

For gcd as a function, the certificate must reach its output gg, giving a lower bound of G(a,b)G(a,b). For an upper bound, append rem(a,g)=z,rem(b,g)=z,eq0(z)=tt,\operatorname{rem}(a,g)=z,\qquad \operatorname{rem}(b,g)=z,\qquad \operatorname{eq}_0(z)=\mathrm{tt}, where the generated zz is zero. Every homomorphism π\pi has π(z)=0\pi(z)=0 and π(g)>0\pi(g)>0 (by definedness of either appended remainder). Thus π(g)\pi(g) divides both input images. Conversely every common divisor of those images divides π(g)\pi(g) by induction over the derivation. Therefore G(a,b)≤callsrem(𝒩0,gcd,a,b)≤G(a,b)+2.G(a,b)\leq\operatorname{calls}_{\operatorname{rem}}(\mathcal N_0,\gcd,a,b)\leq G(a,b)+2. Positivity facts may be appended if the function is restricted to positive inputs. For a noncoprime input, add eq1(g)=ff\operatorname{eq}_1(g)=\mathrm{ff} to this gcd certificate to certify noncoprimeness. Thus the sublogarithmic remainder-call bound holds for both truth values of coprimeness as well as for gcd. Theorem 4F.1 supplies a single uniform process realizing each intrinsic norm pointwise [1, Theorem 4F.1].

Scope and verification

Selecting a close pair of residues is uncharged existential choice in a straight-line derivation. A deterministic remainder-only algorithm cannot assume that choice is available at constant cost. Computing all prefix residues would take about hh calls per contraction. The construction therefore does not settle the deterministic conjecture.

The supplied offline Python planner uses unrestricted arithmetic to select instructions; its independent replay checks operand availability, nonzero divisors, every remainder equation, and the final gcd. Running its default seed reproduces:

A newly specified reproducible suite also passes 310 cases, including 175 noncoprime pairs, with 3,543 contractions across 335 reservoir phases. Four deliberately invalid traces are rejected. This suite is recorded in the repository; it is not claimed to reconstruct the unspecified original 310-case suite.

Machine-checked statements

The Lean 4.35.0 development, with mathlib pinned to version 4.35.0, proves the uniform real-logarithmic bound, its worst-case Big-O formulation, the Euclidean-prefix lemma, the complete three-call contraction lemma, normalization, and the certificate upper bounds. The proof starts from a finite-store instruction semantics in which the only instruction is remainder with a positive, available divisor.
