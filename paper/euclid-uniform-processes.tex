\author{Jason Davies}

\begin{abstract}
Van den Dries and Moschovakis conjectured that the Euclidean algorithm is
optimal, up to a constant factor, among the algorithms that compute the
greatest common divisor from the remainder function. Their homomorphism method
gives lower bounds that hold for every uniform process, deterministic or
nondeterministic. It proves the binary gcd algorithm optimal, but for the
Euclidean setting it has so far produced only $\Omega(\log\log a)$. We show
that here the method cannot reach the conjectured $\Omega(\log a)$. Every pair
$a>b\geq1$ has a remainder-only straight-line derivation of $\gcd(a,b)$ with
$O(\log a/\log\log a)$ remainder calls, where earlier values may be reused but
no constants or other operations are available. The construction combines a
stored Euclidean prefix with three-call contractions and two smaller recursive
derivations; a refinement gives the coefficient $6+o(1)$ when logarithms have
base $2$. Hence the intrinsic remainder-call complexity of the gcd and of
coprimeness in the Euclidean structure is $O(\log a/\log\log a)$, and every
lower bound obtained by the homomorphism method is at most this. The Euclidean
algorithm is therefore not optimal among uniform processes: this refutes
Conjecture~3.4 of Moschovakis's 2012 notes and the precise version (4F-5) of
the Main Conjecture in his book. In the other direction, an elementary
argument gives $\Omega(\log\log a)$ for almost all pairs. We also construct,
for every $\varepsilon>0$, a fixed finite nondeterministic recursive gcd
program with remainder cost at most $\varepsilon\log a+B_\varepsilon$ on all
positive inputs, where cost is the minimum over successful computations.
The program depends on $\varepsilon$; no single sublogarithmic program is
obtained. The Main Conjecture for algorithms, deterministic or
nondeterministic, remains open, and any proof of it must use properties of
algorithms beyond uniformity. The bounds, the certificate reductions, both
refutations, the almost-all lower bound and the finite program family are
formalized in Lean~4, including exact-cost correspondence with a specialized
small-step recursive machine. The final section distinguishes the formalized
results from the cited general theory.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement and relation to the book}
Throughout, logarithms are to base $2$. A derivation starts with its two inputs.
An instruction adds $\rem(x,y)$, where $x,y$ are already available and $y>0$.
Earlier values remain available. No constants are supplied.
Let $G(a,b)$ be the minimum number of instructions needed to make $\gcd(a,b)$
available, and let $D(a,b)=G(a,b)$ for coprime inputs. Thus $G(a,0)=0$ and
$D(a,1)=0$. These are costs of generating values.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Uniformly for integers $a>b\geq1$,
\[
G(a,b)=O\left(\frac{\log a}{\log\log a}\right).
\]
In particular this bound holds for $D(a,b)$ when $\gcd(a,b)=1$.
\end{theorem}
As usual, the asymptotic assertion concerns sufficiently large $a$; a bound
valid at every positive input is $O(1+\log(2+a)/\log\log(4+a))$.

\paragraph{Background.}
Van den Dries and Moschovakis asked whether the Euclidean algorithm is optimal
among its peers, the algorithms that compute the gcd from the remainder
function \cite{vddm2004}. Moschovakis's Main Conjecture asserts that for every
algorithm $\alpha$ from $\rem,\eq_0$ computing $\gcd(x,y)$ there is an $r>0$
such that $\alpha$ makes more than $r\log x$ remainder calls on infinitely many
pairs $x>y\geq1$ \cite[p.~2]{moschovakis2019}.

The known lower bound is a logarithm smaller. For a recursive program deciding
coprimeness from $=$, $<$, $+$, truncated subtraction, $\mathrm{iq}$ and $\rem$,
the depth of nested calls to these primitives exceeds $\tfrac1{10}\log\log a$ on
infinitely many coprime pairs \cite[Theorem~A]{vddm2004}; see also
\cite{vddm2009}. The book proves it as an
intrinsic bound in
$\mathbf N_0[\div]=(\mathbb N,0,1,=,<,+,\dot{-},\mathrm{iq},\rem)$:
$\mathrm{depth}_{\perp\!\!\!\perp}(\mathbf N_0[\div],a,b)>\tfrac1{10}\log\log a$
for every ``difficult pair'', a class that contains every solution of Pell's
equation \cite[Theorem~6C.5, p.~187]{moschovakis2019}, so it
holds for all uniform processes, nondeterministic ones included. A certificate
in $\mathcal N_\varepsilon$ becomes one in $\mathbf N_0[\div]$ when each $\eq_0$
or $\eq_1$ fact is replaced by an equality with the constant $0$ or $1$, and its
depth then exceeds its number of remainder facts by at most a constant. Hence
$\calls_{\rem}(\mathcal N_\varepsilon,\perp\!\!\!\perp,a,b)=\Omega(\log\log a)$ on
all difficult pairs. By contrast, for the binary gcd algorithm the same method
gives a matching logarithmic lower bound: a recursive program deciding
coprimeness from $=$, $<$, $+$, truncated subtraction, $2x$, $\mathrm{iq}(x,2)$
and $\mathrm{rem}(x,2)$ needs more than $\tfrac1{10}\log(a^2-1)$ calls on the
pair $(a,a^2-1)$, for every $a>2$ \cite[Theorem~B]{vddm2004}. Pratt's nondeterministic algorithm
\cite[Theorem~2E.2 and Problems x2E.17--x2E.19]{moschovakis2019} matches this
bound up to a constant factor on successive Fibonacci numbers
\cite[Corollary~6C.7]{moschovakis2019}. Whether a logarithmic bound holds for
all uniform processes was left open \cite[p.~188]{moschovakis2019},
\cite[Conjecture~3.4]{moschovakis2012}.

In a different model, Bshouty showed that a random access machine with linear
operations, shifts and indirect addressing computes the gcd of two integers in
$[0,N]$ in $O(\log N/\log\log N)$ operations, by reading precomputed blocks of the binary
gcd algorithm from a table. He also proved that this is optimal: even with
rational constants, multiplication and division by constants, and the floor
function added, $\Omega(\log N/\log\log N)$ operations are necessary
\cite[Theorems~i and~ii]{bshouty1989}. The construction here uses no
operation other than remainder and no table. Its saving of a factor
$\log\log a$ comes instead from reusing one stored Euclidean prefix for every
modulus.

\paragraph{The conjectures refuted.}
The definitions of generated certificates and intrinsic complexities are in
\cite[\S\S1D, 4C--4E]{moschovakis2019} and are recalled in
Section~\ref{sec:certificates}. Write
$\mathcal N_0=(\mathbb N,\rem,\eq_0)$ and
$\mathcal N_\varepsilon=(\mathbb N,\rem,\eq_0,\eq_1)$; the structure
$\mathcal N_0$ is not the book's $\mathbf N_0$, whose expansion
$\mathbf N_0[\div]$ appears above. Write $\calls_{\rem}(\mathcal A,f,\vec x)$ for the least number of remainder facts in
a certificate for $f$ at $\vec x$ in the structure $\mathcal A$. Coprimeness is
the relation $x\perp\!\!\!\perp y\iff x,y\geq1\wedge\gcd(x,y)=1$
\cite[\S1A]{moschovakis2019}, \cite{moschovakis2012}. Moschovakis's 2012 notes
state:
\begin{quote}
\textbf{Conjecture 3.4} \cite{moschovakis2012}. There is a rational number
$r>0$ such that for infinitely many $a\geq b\geq1$,
$\calls_{\rem}(\mathcal N_\varepsilon,\perp\!\!\!\perp,a,b)\geq r\log a$.
\end{quote}
The book formulates one precise version of the Main Conjecture
\cite[p.~151]{moschovakis2019}: there are a set $\mathcal D\subseteq\mathbb N^2$
and a $K>0$ such that
\[
(x,y)\in\mathcal D\ \Longrightarrow\
\calls_{\rem}(\varepsilon,x,y)\leq L(x,y)\leq K\calls_{\rem}(\mathcal A,\gcd,x,y),
\tag{4F-5}
\]
where $L(x,y)=2\log\max(x,y)$ and $\varepsilon$ is the Euclidean algorithm.
The book adds that $\mathcal D$ then satisfies its conditions (4F-3) and
(4F-4). Condition (4F-4) makes the intrinsic complexity unbounded on
$\mathcal D$, so the intended $\mathcal D$ is infinite; for finite $\mathcal D$
the statement would be trivial. Dropping the first inequality and asking only
for an infinite $\mathcal D$ gives a weaker statement, and we refute that
weaker statement.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:refute}
The intrinsic complexities
\[
\calls_{\rem}(\mathcal N_\varepsilon,\perp\!\!\!\perp,a,b),\qquad
\calls_{\rem}(\mathcal N_0,\gcd,a,b),\qquad
\calls_{\rem}(\mathcal N_\varepsilon,\gcd,a,b)
\]
are $O(\log a/\log\log a)$, uniformly for $a\geq b\geq1$. Consequently Conjecture~3.4 of
\cite{moschovakis2012} is false, and so is the version (4F-5) of the Main
Conjecture with $\mathcal A=\mathcal N_0$ or $\mathcal A=\mathcal N_\varepsilon$:
for every $r>0$, respectively $K>0$, only finitely many pairs satisfy the stated
inequality.
\end{corollary}
The corollary follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and
Section~\ref{sec:certificates}. For (4F-5) it covers all pairs in
$\mathbb N^2$: the gcd is symmetric, and a pair with a zero entry is certified by
the single fact $\eq_0(0)=\mathrm{tt}$, with no remainder facts. It is
formalized as \lean{ShortGCD.not_conjecture_3_4},
\lean{ShortGCD.not_main_conjecture_4F5} (for $\mathcal N_0$) and
\lean{ShortGCD.not_main_conjecture_4F5_Neps} (for $\mathcal N_\varepsilon$).
The literal readings, with a rational $r$ and infinitely many $a$ for
Conjecture~3.4, and with a set $\mathcal D$ on which the complexity is unbounded
for (4F-5), are \lean{ShortGCD.not_conjecture_3_4_literal} and
\lean{ShortGCD.not_main_conjecture_4F5_literal}.

Theorem~4F.1 \cite[pp.~149--150]{moschovakis2019} supplies a single uniform
process that attains these pointwise intrinsic bounds. So some uniform process
of $\mathcal N_\varepsilon$ decides coprimeness, and one computes the gcd, with
$O(\log a/\log\log a)$ remainder calls on all pairs $a\geq b\geq1$. This settles
the possibility
raised after Corollary~6C.7 \cite[p.~188]{moschovakis2019}: the Main Conjecture
does not hold for all uniform processes of $\mathcal N_\varepsilon$.

\paragraph{The limit of the homomorphism method.}
The homomorphism method proves lower bounds on intrinsic complexities. These
bound every uniform process that computes the function: if $\alpha$ computes
$f$ in $\mathcal A$, then $\mu(\mathcal A,f,\vec x)\leq\mu(\alpha,\vec x)$,
in particular for every deterministic or nondeterministic recursive program
\cite[Proposition~4E.1, Corollary~4E.2]{moschovakis2019}. The Homomorphism
Test \cite[Lemma~4E.3]{moschovakis2019} proves a lower bound on
$\mu(\mathcal A,f,\vec x)$ itself, by showing that every generated diagram
below the proposed cost admits a homomorphism that fails to preserve the
answer. Corollary~\ref{cor:refute} caps every such argument.

\begin{corollary}[Limit of the homomorphism method]\label{cor:method}
Let $g(a,b)$ be any lower bound for
$\calls_{\rem}(\mathcal N_\varepsilon,\perp\!\!\!\perp,a,b)$, or for
$\calls_{\rem}(\mathcal A,\gcd,a,b)$ with $\mathcal A=\mathcal N_0$ or
$\mathcal N_\varepsilon$, for instance one obtained from the Homomorphism Test.
Then $g(a,b)=O(\log a/\log\log a)$, uniformly for $a\geq b\geq1$. In
particular, no lower bound on intrinsic complexity shows that every algorithm
from $\rem,\eq_0$ computing the gcd makes at least $r\log a$ remainder calls
on infinitely many pairs. A proof of the Main Conjecture must use properties
of algorithms that uniform processes need not have, such as being induced by a
finite program, or determinism.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Immediate from Corollary~\ref{cor:refute}.
\end{proof}
The corollary does not exclude arguments that combine homomorphisms with
additional restrictions on computations.

Corollaries~\ref{cor:refute} and~\ref{cor:method} do not settle the Main
Conjecture for algorithms, that is,
recursive programs, deterministic or nondeterministic
\cite[p.~2]{moschovakis2019}. Nor do they settle the form for deterministic
uniform processes \cite[Conjecture~3.9]{moschovakis2012},
\cite[\S4H and p.~188]{moschovakis2019}; see Section~\ref{sec:scope}. With the lower bound above, the intrinsic complexity
of coprimeness in $\mathcal N_\varepsilon$ now lies between $\Omega(\log\log a)$,
on infinitely many pairs and in fact on almost all pairs
(Proposition~\ref{prop:loglog}), and $O(\log a/\log\log a)$, on all pairs. Closing this
gap is an open problem. In particular, it is not known whether the bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is optimal for remainder-only derivations, as the
corresponding bound is in Bshouty's model.

\paragraph{A counting hypothesis.}
Both open questions reduce to one counting problem, treated in the
accompanying note \lean{research/shape-count.tex}. Name each operand of a
remainder-only derivation by its position rather than its value; call the
result a \emph{shape}. A fixed recursive program, deterministic or
nondeterministic, produces at most $C_P^{\,c+1}$ shapes among its computations
with at most $c$ remainder calls, whereas derivations of length $c$ have
$((c+1)!)^2$. Hence, if every shape of length at most $c_0\log N$ reaches $1$
on at most $N^{2-\delta}$ pairs $2\leq b<a\leq N$, then every fixed program
computing the gcd or deciding coprimeness makes at least $r_P\log a$ remainder
calls on almost all coprime pairs, and Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is tight on
almost all pairs. The shape-count bound and both reductions are formalized in
Lean (\lean{ShortGCD.GeneralPrograms}, \lean{ShortGCD.ShapeCount},
\lean{ShortGCD.TightnessReduction}); the counting hypothesis itself is open.

There is also an algorithmic consequence with a different quantifier order.
Section~\ref{sec:programs} constructs fixed finite nondeterministic recursive
programs with arbitrarily small logarithmic remainder-call coefficients,
uniformly over all inputs. The programs use a fixed-length prefix and ordinary
recursive calls. This complements Pratt's bound on successive Fibonacci
numbers: the saving here is uniform, while the coefficient is chosen by
enlarging the program. The Main Conjecture allows its lower-bound coefficient
to depend on the program, so this consequence is compatible with it.

\section{Normalization}
Write $g=\gcd(a,b)>0$. For positive $y$,
\[
\rem(gx,gy)=g\rem(x,y).
\]
Every generated value is divisible by $g$. Consequently scaling a derivation,
or dividing all its values by $g$, preserves its length and the validity of
every instruction. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normalize}
G(a,b)=D(a/g,b/g).
\end{equation}
Define
\[
T(n)=\max_{0\leq b\leq a\leq 2^n,\ a>0}G(a,b).
\]
Euclid's algorithm makes this a finite maximum. Normalization reduces an upper
bound for $T$ to coprime inputs, with equality, zero inputs, and inputs equal to
one handled without calls.

\section{Three-call contractions}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:gap}
If $m>0$ is available and a finite collection $S$ of available values occupies
$t\geq2$ residue classes modulo $m$, at most three remainder calls generate $w$
with
\[
0<w<\frac{m}{t-1}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Sort the distinct residues. Some adjacent pair $u<v$ has gap
$\delta=v-u<m/(t-1)$, because the sum of the $t-1$ adjacent gaps is less than
$m$. Each selected residue can be generated with one call from a witness in
$S$. If $u=0$, use $v$. If $0<u\leq\delta$, use $u$. Otherwise
$0<\delta<u$ and $v=u+\delta$, so $\rem(v,u)=\delta$. This uses at most three
calls. Subtraction and sorting describe the proof and the selection of
instructions; they are not instructions of the derivation.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Euclidean-prefix lemma]\label{lem:prefix}
Let $r_0=a>r_1=b>\cdots>r_h>0$ be a Euclidean prefix of a coprime pair, with
$r_{i-1}=q_i r_i+r_{i+1}$ and $q_i\geq1$ for $1\leq i<h$. For every integer
$m>2a/r_h$, the
$h$ pairs
\[
(r_i\bmod m,r_{i+1}\bmod m),\qquad 0\leq i<h,
\]
are distinct. The prefix therefore occupies $t$ residue classes with $t^2\geq h$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose the pairs at $i<j$ coincide. Set
\[
M=\prod_{\ell=i+1}^{j}\begin{pmatrix}q_\ell&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
 =\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\C&E\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\binom{r_i}{r_{i+1}}=M\binom{r_j}{r_{j+1}}.
\]
Right multiplication by
$\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}q&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ sends
$(A,B,C,E)$ to $(qA+B,\,A,\,qC+E,\,C)$, starting from $(q,1,1,0)$ for a single
factor. By induction, all entries are nonnegative, $A\geq B\geq E$,
$A\geq C\geq1$, $B\geq1$, and $\det M=(-1)^{j-i}$.
Also $Ar_j\leq r_i\leq a$, whence $A\leq a/r_h$.
Write
\[
\Delta=\det(M-I)=1+\det M-A-E.
\]
For an odd number of factors, $\Delta=-A-E<0$. By the same recursion, a
product of at least two factors has $A\geq2$ and $E\geq1$; thus for an even
positive number of factors,
$\Delta=2-A-E<0$. In either case,
\[
0<|\Delta|\leq A+E\leq2A\leq2a/r_h<m.
\]
The presumed congruence gives $(M-I)(r_j,r_{j+1})^{\mathsf T}\equiv0\pmod m$.
Multiplication by the adjugate shows that $m$ divides both $\Delta r_j$ and
$\Delta r_{j+1}$. Consecutive remainders are coprime, so B\'ezout's identity
implies $m\mid\Delta$, a contradiction. Finally, $t$ individual residue classes
give at most $t^2$ ordered pairs.
\end{proof}
Thus a stored prefix can be reused for every available modulus $m>2a/r_h$.
When $\sqrt h>1$, three calls suffice to generate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:contract}
0<w<\frac{m}{\sqrt h-1}.
\end{equation}
The universal quantifier over $m$ is essential: the modulus generated by a
contraction is not predetermined.

\section{Construction and recurrence}
Fix an integer $n>16$ and put $N=2^n$ and $h=\lceil\sqrt n\rceil$, so that $h>4$.
After normalization we may assume $a>b>1$ and $a\leq N$.
Generate the Euclidean prefix until its last positive value is at most
$N^{3/4}$ or until $r_h$ is available. Check the input $b$ as part of this
rule. The preliminary work costs at most $h$ calls. Euclid cannot encounter
zero first without having encountered the positive remainder $1$.

If a positive remainder $c\leq N^{3/4}$ is encountered, generate the next
remainder $s<c$ with one more call. The pair $(c,s)$ is coprime. If $s=0$, then
$c=1$ is already available; otherwise both inputs to the remaining derivation
are at most $2^{\lceil3n/4\rceil}$. This gives the bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:early}
h+1+T(\lceil3n/4\rceil).
\end{equation}

Otherwise $r_h>N^{3/4}$, so $2a/r_h<2N^{1/4}$. For $n>12$,
$N^{1/3}>2N^{1/4}$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:prefix} applies to every available
$m>N^{1/3}$. Starting at $m=r_h$, repeatedly use the contraction inequality until
$0<m\leq N^{1/3}$. Set
\[
\kappa_n=\sqrt h-1>1,\qquad
J_n=\left\lceil\frac{2n/3}{\log\kappa_n}\right\rceil.
\]
There are at most $J_n$ contractions: if $J_n$ have occurred then
$m<N/\kappa_n^{J_n}\leq N^{1/3}$. Since
$\log\kappa_n=\tfrac14\log n+O(1)$, their cost is $3J_n=O(n/\log n)$.

The small $m$ need not be coprime to either input. Generate
$u=\rem(a,m)$ and $v=\rem(b,m)$, then concatenate derivations of
$d=\gcd(m,u)$ and $e=\gcd(d,v)$. These derivations exist by the definition of
$T$, since $G$ is symmetric in its arguments, and use only inputs bounded by $m$.
They are not gcd-oracle instructions.
The gcd identities give
\[
e=\gcd(\gcd(m,a\bmod m),b\bmod m)
 =\gcd(m,\gcd(a,b))=1.
\]
The cost in this case is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:late}
h+3J_n+2+2T(\lceil n/3\rceil).
\end{equation}
Retaining values makes concatenation legitimate. In particular, the second
recursive derivation has both $d$ and $v$ available even if the first one uses
neither original input.

The two case estimates and normalization imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recurrence}
T(n)\leq\max\{T(\lceil3n/4\rceil)+O(\sqrt n),\;
2T(\lceil n/3\rceil)+O(n/\log n)\}.
\end{equation}
All implied constants are independent of the inputs.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
For $n\geq2$ let $f(n)=n/\log n$. The ratios
\[
\frac{f(\lceil3n/4\rceil)}{f(n)}\longrightarrow\frac34,
\qquad
\frac{2f(\lceil n/3\rceil)}{f(n)}\longrightarrow\frac23
\]
show that, beyond a fixed threshold, $f(\lceil3n/4\rceil)\leq\tfrac78f(n)$ and
$2f(\lceil n/3\rceil)\leq\tfrac56f(n)$. Both overheads in
(\ref{eq:recurrence}) are at most $c_0f(n)$ for a fixed $c_0$. Choose the
induction threshold above $16$, where the recurrence holds, and large enough
that both recursive indices are at least $2$ and strictly less than $n$. Choose
$C\geq8c_0$, increased to cover the finitely many values below the threshold.
Strong induction then gives $T(n)\leq Cf(n)$: the two bounds are
$(7C/8+c_0)f(n)$ and $(5C/6+c_0)f(n)$, both at most $Cf(n)$.
Finally take $n=\lceil\log a\rceil$. This also gives $G(a,b)=o(\log a)$
uniformly as $a\to\infty$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The leading coefficient}
The same construction allows a more precise bound without changing either
arithmetic lemma.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:six}
For every $\varepsilon>0$, there is an $a_0$ such that for every
$a\geq a_0$ and $0\leq b\leq a$,
\[
G(a,b)\leq(6+\varepsilon)\frac{\log a}{\log\log a}.
\]
Equivalently, the uniform upper bound has coefficient $6+o(1)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Fix $0<c<1/2$ and $0<\beta<1/2$, and put $\alpha=1-\beta/2$.
For sufficiently large $n$, choose
\[
p=\lfloor c\log n\rfloor,\qquad k=2^p,\qquad h=k^2+1,
\qquad J=\lceil n/p\rceil.
\]
Use $N^\alpha$ as the early stopping threshold and $N^\beta$ as the
small-modulus threshold, where $N=2^n$. Since $\alpha+\beta>1$,
the prefix lemma applies to every modulus above $N^\beta$ for sufficiently
large $n$. Since $k^2<h$, the prefix occupies at least $k+1$ residue classes,
so each contraction reduces the modulus by a factor greater than $k$.
Moreover $k^J\geq N$, so $J$ contractions suffice.
Writing $f(n)=n/\log n$, we have
\[
h=O(n^{2c})=o(f(n)),\qquad
3J=\left(\frac3c+o(1)\right)f(n).
\]
The two branches therefore give
\[
T(n)\leq\max\left\{
 T(\lceil\alpha n\rceil)+o(f(n)),\quad
 2T(\lceil\beta n\rceil)+\left(\frac3c+o(1)\right)f(n)
\right\}.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} implies that
$L=\limsup_{n\to\infty}T(n)/f(n)$ is finite and nonnegative.
For each fixed $q>0$, the ratio $f(\lceil qn\rceil)/f(n)$ tends to $q$.
Taking upper limits in the recurrence yields
\[
L\leq\max\{\alpha L,\ 2\beta L+3/c\}.
\]
As $\alpha<1$, this implies $L(1-2\beta)\leq3/c$; the case $L=0$ is
immediate. First let $\beta\downarrow0$ and then $c\uparrow1/2$ to obtain
$L\leq6$. Finally, $n=\lceil\log a\rceil$ satisfies
$f(n)\sim\log a/\log\log a$, proving the assertion. All upper limits concern
the worst-case function $T$, so the resulting threshold is independent of $b$.
\end{proof}
No optimality of the coefficient $6$ is asserted. The refinement is formalized
as \lean{ShortGCD.uniform_real_bound_six}; the original fixed parameters suffice
for the simpler Big-O proof above.

\section{Certificates and uniform processes}\label{sec:certificates}
Use the book's partial remainder operation: its second argument must be
nonzero. Recall $\mathcal N_0=(\mathbb N,\rem,\eq_0)$ and
$\mathcal N_\varepsilon=(\mathbb N,\rem,\eq_0,\eq_1)$; compare
\cite[(1C-10), pp.~24, 142--143]{moschovakis2019}.
A derivation determines a finite generated partial substructure by recording
its remainder equations. Repeated equations can be discarded.

Consider first the relation $\gcd(a,b)=1$ on all natural inputs. A derivation of $1$
together with $\eq_1(1)=\mathrm{tt}$ certifies the relation: every common
divisor of the images of the inputs divides every transported generated
value, hence divides $1$. Conversely, without a positive $\eq_1$ fact the
map $x\mapsto2x$ preserves every recorded remainder and zero-test fact, as
well as every negative one-test fact. It cannot preserve coprimeness. A
certificate therefore reaches $1$. Enumerating a finite generated diagram
in generation order gives a derivation using no more calls than the number
of remainder facts. Consequently, on coprime inputs,
\[
\calls_{\rem}(\mathcal N_\varepsilon,\gcd{=}1,a,b)=D(a,b),\qquad
\calls(\mathcal N_\varepsilon,\gcd{=}1,a,b)=D(a,b)+1.
\]
For the book's relation $\perp\!\!\!\perp$, which also requires $a,b\geq1$, it
suffices to add the facts $\eq_0(a)=\mathrm{ff}$ and $\eq_0(b)=\mathrm{ff}$.
Conversely, when $a\perp\!\!\!\perp b$, every certificate for
$\perp\!\!\!\perp$ also certifies $\gcd(a,b)=1$. So for coprime $a,b\geq1$ the
remainder count is unchanged,
$\calls_{\rem}(\mathcal N_\varepsilon,\perp\!\!\!\perp,a,b)=D(a,b)$, and the
total count is at most $D(a,b)+3$. For noncoprime inputs $D$ plays no role;
the bound there comes from the gcd certificate below.

For gcd as a function, the certificate must reach its output $g$, giving a
lower bound of $G(a,b)$. For an upper bound, append
\[
\rem(a,g)=z,\qquad \rem(b,g)=z,\qquad \eq_0(z)=\mathrm{tt},
\]
where the generated $z$ is zero. Every homomorphism $\pi$ has $\pi(z)=0$ and
$\pi(g)>0$ (by definedness of either appended remainder). Thus $\pi(g)$
divides both input images. Conversely every common divisor of those images
divides $\pi(g)$ by induction over the derivation. Therefore, for
$(a,b)\neq(0,0)$,
\[
G(a,b)\leq\calls_{\rem}(\mathcal N_0,\gcd,a,b)\leq G(a,b)+2.
\]
At $(0,0)$ the output is an input and the empty certificate suffices.
Positivity facts may be appended if the function is restricted to positive
inputs. For a noncoprime input, add $\eq_1(g)=\mathrm{ff}$ to this gcd
certificate to certify noncoprimeness. Thus the sublogarithmic remainder-call
bound holds for both truth values of coprimeness as well as for gcd.
The fixed certificate overhead does not change the leading coefficient.
Thus Corollary~\ref{cor:six} also holds for each of the three intrinsic
complexities in Corollary~\ref{cor:refute}, on positive inputs. This is
\lean{ShortGCD.uniform_intrinsic_bound_six}.
Theorem~4F.1 supplies a single uniform process realizing each intrinsic norm
pointwise \cite[Theorem~4F.1]{moschovakis2019}.

\section{Fixed programs with arbitrarily small coefficients}\label{sec:programs}

For a nondeterministic program $P$, let $c_P(a,b)$ be the minimum number of
remainder calls among its convergent computations. We require at least one
such computation on each input and require every convergent computation to
return the gcd. These are the conventions of
\cite[(2E-2), (2E-5)]{moschovakis2019}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:programs}
For each integer $p\geq1$, there is a finite nondeterministic recursive program
$P_p$ over $(\mathbb N,\rem,\eq_0)$ computing the gcd such that
\[
\limsup_{a\to\infty}\ \max_{1\leq b\leq a}
\frac{c_{P_p}(a,b)}{\log a}\leq\frac3p.
\]
Its generator has $W=4^p+5$ individual parameters, and no internal function
has larger arity. Consequently, for every $\varepsilon>0$ there are a fixed
program $P$ and a constant $B\geq0$ such that
\[
\forall a\geq b\geq1,\qquad c_P(a,b)\leq\varepsilon\log a+B.
\]
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{The program.}
Fix $W\geq3$ and write $\vec x=(x_0,\ldots,x_{W-1})$ and
$\vec x[i\leftarrow E]$ for the tuple with its $i$th expression replaced by
$E$. The generator is given by the finite alternatives
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{choose}(\vec x)&=x_i &&(0\leq i<W),\\
\operatorname{gen}(\vec x)&=\operatorname{choose}(\vec x),\\
\operatorname{gen}(\vec x)&=\operatorname{gen}
  (\vec x[i\leftarrow\rem(\operatorname{choose}(\vec x),
                               \operatorname{choose}(\vec x))])
  &&(0\leq i<W),\\
\operatorname{gen}(\vec x)&=\operatorname{gen}
  (\vec x[i\leftarrow\operatorname{gen}(\vec x)]) &&(0\leq i<W),\\
\operatorname{gen}(\vec x)&=\operatorname{gen}
  (\operatorname{choose}(\vec x),\ldots,\operatorname{choose}(\vec x)).
\end{align*}
There are $W$ independent selector calls in the last line. Tuple notation and
indices are syntax abbreviations: after fixing $W$, each line expands into
ordinary first-order terms with fixed arities. No tuple, index, numeric
constant, or update operation is supplied as runtime data or a primitive.
The nested generator in the fourth line is evaluated once; its caller's
other parameters remain available when it returns. The recursion stack can
grow, so $W$ is an arity bound, not a total storage bound.

Add $\operatorname{loop}(z)=\operatorname{loop}(z)$ and define
$\operatorname{accept}(a,b,g)$ by nested zero-test conditionals: if $g=0$,
loop; otherwise test $\rem(a,g)=0$ and then $\rem(b,g)=0$, returning $g$ if
both tests succeed and looping otherwise. The head is
\[
P_W(a,b)=\text{if }\eq_0(b)\text{ then }a\text{ else }
\operatorname{accept}(a,b,\operatorname{gen}(a,b,a,\ldots,a)).
\]
The four internal symbols have arities $W,W,3,1$. Every common divisor of
the generator's parameters divides its output, by induction on a finite
evaluation. Thus a candidate is divisible by $\gcd(a,b)$; successful
verification proves the reverse divisibility. Every convergent computation
therefore returns the gcd. There is always a convergent computation following
Euclid, using parameter selection and copying to retain the current pair.
For $b>0$, final verification costs exactly two remainder calls.

\paragraph{Operational correspondence.}
The syntax above is a finite recursive program in the sense of
\cite[\S\S2A, 2E]{moschovakis2019}. In Lean we specialize the book's
two-stack transition table to this language and program family. Closures
represent substitution of actual values into terms; they are machine states,
not additional program data. The control stack has its top at the head, and
the value stack distinguishes individuals from booleans. Argument evaluation,
zero testing, and branching are explicit transitions. The finite evaluation
trees correspond to convergent machine computations, preserving both result
and remainder count. For the forward direction, induct on the evaluation
tree. Evaluate argument subtrees right to left, as prescribed by the machine's
composition rule, then choose the indicated body at the internal call.
Transition locality permits each subtree computation to be placed in its
calling context \cite[Table~1 and Lemma~2D.1, pp.~75--76]{moschovakis2019}.
A remainder node adds one external remainder call; a conditional evaluates
its guard, makes one uncharged zero test, and evaluates only the selected
branch. Variables and internal calls add no remainder calls. For the reverse
direction, decompose a convergent computation at the completed argument
evaluations and the selected body or branch, and induct on its length.
This gives the same additive cost equations. Choices belong to individual
call occurrences, and the language has no shared mutable state, so serializing
the argument trees introduces no constraint on their choices. No successful
tree uses a zero divisor or terminates through $\operatorname{loop}$.
The exact-cost equivalence is checked as
\lean{ShortGCD.RegisterProgram.Syntax.Machine.eval_iff_steps}; transition
locality is checked as \lean{Machine.Step.context} in the same namespace.
This formalizes the machine needed here, not the book's theory for arbitrary
structures and program vocabularies.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:programs}]
Set $k=2^p$, $h=k^2+1$, and $W=h+4$, and take $P_p=P_W$.
Store $r_0,\ldots,r_h$ in $h+1$
parameters, reserving three parameters for the modulus and two temporary
residues. Lemmas~\ref{lem:gap} and~\ref{lem:prefix} give a contraction by a
factor greater than $k$ in at most three remainder calls, preserving the
prefix. The program can choose precisely the operands that witness each
contraction.

Let $T_W(n)$ be the maximum, over $a,b\leq2^n$, of the minimum generator
cost to return $\gcd(a,b)$ from $(a,b,a,\ldots,a)$. Euclid gives
$T_W(n)=O(n)$. Copying parameters gives symmetry, and positive scaling
preserves generator runs and their remainder counts. It therefore suffices
to analyze coprime inputs.

Fix $0<\beta<1/2$ and put $\alpha=1-\beta/2$. Use the early threshold
$N^\alpha$ and the target modulus $N^\beta$, where $N=2^n$. If the prefix
is large, $2a/r_h<2N^{1-\alpha}<N^\beta$ for all sufficiently large $n$.
There are at least $k+1$ distinct prefix residues modulo every modulus used
before stopping, since $h>k^2$. At most $\lceil n/p\rceil$ contractions
produce a positive $m\leq N^\beta$.

Form $u=a\bmod m$ and $v=b\bmod m$ in the temporary positions. A nested
generator call, with its parameters routed to copies of $m,u$, returns
$d=\gcd(m,u)$ while its caller retains $v$. Replace the modulus parameter
by $d$ and route to copies of $d,v$ for the second gcd computation. The
result is $\gcd(m,a,b)=1$. For general inputs the scaled witness returns
the original gcd. In the early case, route directly to the smaller
consecutive Euclidean pair. All these routes and nested calls are available
in the same fixed program. Thus, eventually in $n$,
\[
T_W(n)\leq\max\left\{
 T_W(\lceil\alpha n\rceil)+h+1,\quad
 2T_W(\lceil\beta n\rceil)+h+3\lceil n/p\rceil+2
\right\}.
\]
Set $L_W=\limsup_{n\to\infty}T_W(n)/n$, finite and nonnegative by Euclid.
Since $h$ is fixed, division by $n$ and passage to upper limits gives
\[
L_W\leq\max\{\alpha L_W,\ 2\beta L_W+3/p\}.
\]
As $\alpha<1$, this implies $(1-2\beta)L_W\leq3/p$. The program is
independent of $\beta$, so letting $\beta\downarrow0$ gives $L_W\leq3/p$.
Take $n=\lceil\log a\rceil$ and add the two verification calls. Finally,
choose $p>3/\varepsilon$ and absorb the finitely many smaller inputs in $B$.
\end{proof}

The theorem is a statement about a family of programs. The prefix length
grows when a smaller coefficient is requested, but remains fixed as the
inputs grow. It does not exchange $\forall\varepsilon\,\exists P_\varepsilon$
with $\exists P\,\forall\varepsilon$. Neither the coefficient nor the arity
bound is claimed optimal. Merely inlining several remainder instructions
into one term would still charge for each external remainder call; the saving
here uses the prefix lemma and recursive reduction.

\section{An almost-all lower bound}\label{sec:loglog}
The difficult pairs of \cite[Theorem~6C.5]{moschovakis2019} give
$\Omega(\log\log a)$ on a sparse set. An elementary Diophantine argument gives
the same order for almost all pairs, simultaneously for every derivation.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:loglog}
For every $d\geq1$ and $N\geq3$, at most $16(2+\ln N)N^{2-2^{-d}}$ pairs
$2\leq b<a\leq N$ satisfy $D(a,b)\leq d$. Hence
$D(a,b)\geq(1-o(1))\log\log N$ for all but $o(N^2)$ coprime pairs below $N$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $B\geq1$ call $(a,b)$ \emph{$B$-regular} if $|qb-pa|\geq a/(Bq)$ whenever
$q\geq1$, $p\in\mathbb Z$ and $qb\neq pa$.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Small values have large coefficients.} Every generated value is
an integer form $v=ua+wb$. If $(a,b)$ is $B$-regular and $v>0$, then
$v\geq a/(B(|u|+|w|))$: for $w\neq0$ apply regularity with $q=|w|$, and for
$w=0$ note $v\geq a$.
\item \textbf{Coefficients grow at most doubly exponentially.} Let $H_t$ bound
$|u|+|w|$ over the values available after $t$ steps, so $H_0=1$. A step
$x=qy+r$ has $q\leq a/y\leq BH_t$, hence $H_{t+1}\leq H_t+BH_t^2\leq2BH_t^2$
and $H_t\leq(2B)^{2^t-1}$.
\item \textbf{Regular pairs need long derivations.} Producing $1$ within $d$
steps forces $1\geq a/(BH_d)$, that is $a\leq(2B)^{2^d}/2$.
\item \textbf{Few pairs are irregular.} Suppose $0<|qb-pa|<a/(Bq)$. Dividing
$p,q$ by their gcd preserves this, and it forces $q<a/B\leq N/B$ and
$0\leq p\leq q$. For each $q$ there are at most $2q$ values of $p$ and
$2N/(Bq)$ values of $r=qb-pa$. Given $p,q,r$ with $\gcd(p,q)=1$, $a$ lies in
one residue class modulo $q$, which leaves at most $N/q+1$ choices, and $b$ is
then determined. So at most $(4N^2/B)(2+\ln N)$ pairs below $N$ are irregular.
\item \textbf{Dyadic ranges.} For $T/2<a\leq T$ take $B=T^{2^{-d}}/2$. If
$B\geq1$, a regular pair with $D\leq d$ would have $a\leq T/2$, so every pair
in the range with $D\leq d$ is irregular. That gives at most
$8(2+\ln T)T^{2-2^{-d}}$ pairs, and the same bound is trivial when $B<1$.
Summing over $T=N,N/2,\ldots$ gives the first claim.
\end{itemize}
For the second claim take $d=\lfloor\log_2\ln N-2\log_2\ln\ln N\rfloor$. Then
$N^{-2^{-d}}\leq\exp(-(\ln\ln N)^2)$, so the bound is $o(N^2)$. \qedhere
\end{proof}
Lean checks Proposition~\ref{prop:loglog} with the constant $40(2+\ln N)$ in
place of $16(2+\ln N)$: it takes $B=a^{2^{-d}}/2$ separately for each pair
instead of dyadic ranges (\lean{ShortGCD.AlmostAll.card_within_le},
\lean{ShortGCD.AlmostAll.card_D_le}), and it proves the second claim in the
form $D(a,b)>(1-\varepsilon)\log_2\log_2N$ for all but $o(N^2)$ pairs, for
every $\varepsilon\in(0,1]$ (\lean{ShortGCD.AlmostAll.tendsto_card_within_loglog}).
The saving $2^{-d}$ shrinks as $d$ grows, so this argument cannot reach the
scale $\log N/\log\log N$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, let alone $\log N$.

\section{Scope and verification}\label{sec:scope}
Selecting a close pair of residues is uncharged existential choice in a
straight-line derivation. A deterministic remainder-only algorithm cannot
assume that choice is available at constant cost. Computing all prefix
residues would take about $h$ calls per contraction. Nondeterministic operand
selection and the fixed-prefix programs of Section~\ref{sec:programs} leave a
further question: can one fixed first-order recursive
program retain and repeatedly access the growing prefix within the same
remainder-call bound? Proposition~2E.1 converts each certificate into a
computation of some program; it does not supply one program for all
certificates \cite[\S2E]{moschovakis2019}. The book also distinguishes the
best uniform process from a nondeterministic program \cite[p.~150]{moschovakis2019}.
The construction therefore settles neither algorithmic version of the Main
Conjecture.

The supplied offline Python planner uses the original construction parameters
and unrestricted arithmetic to select instructions; its independent replay checks operand availability, nonzero
divisors, every remainder equation, and the final gcd. Below a base size
(256 bits by default) it uses Euclid's algorithm. On one pseudorandom coprime
pair of each size, generated from its default seed, it gives:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{rrr}\toprule
Input bits & Derivation calls & Euclid calls to reach $1$, same pair\\\midrule
512 & 196 & 326\\
2048 & 669 & 1204\\
8192 & 2059 & 4754\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Random pairs are not Euclid's worst case: on successive Fibonacci numbers
Euclid needs about $1.44\log a$ calls. These are checks of finite instances,
not an asymptotic proof. A reproducible suite of 310 cases also passes,
including 175 noncoprime pairs, with base size 64 bits and 3,543
contractions in total, and four deliberately invalid traces are rejected. The
Euclidean-prefix lemma was also checked exhaustively for every coprime pair
with $3\leq a<1500$ and $1\leq b<a$, every prefix length, and up to 60
consecutive moduli above the threshold $2a/r_h$, restricted to $m<4a$
(254,722,179 instances, no failures).

\subsection{Machine-checked statements}
The Lean 4.35.0 development, with mathlib pinned to version 4.35.0, proves the
uniform real-logarithmic bound, its worst-case Big-O formulation, the leading
coefficient in Corollary~\ref{cor:six}, the Euclidean-prefix lemma, the complete three-call contraction lemma, normalization,
and the certificate upper bounds. It contains no proof placeholders or added
axioms. An axiom audit of the main results reports only the standard Lean
foundations \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, and \texttt{Quot.sound}.
The theorem \lean{ShortGCD.uniform_real_bound} states
\[
\exists C>0\ \forall a,b\in\mathbb N,\quad
4\leq a\ \wedge\ b\leq a\ \Longrightarrow\
G(a,b)\leq C\frac{\log_2 a}{\log_2(\log_2 a)}.
\]
The sharper theorem \lean{ShortGCD.uniform_real_bound_six} states that for
every $\varepsilon>0$, eventually in $a$, the same inequality holds for all
$b\leq a$ with $C=6+\varepsilon$.
\lean{ShortGCD.uniform_intrinsic_bound_six} gives the corresponding statement
for the three intrinsic complexities on positive inputs.
The proof starts from a finite-store instruction semantics in which the only
instruction is remainder with a positive, available divisor. Costs are indices
of derivations, rather than an assumed recurrence. A formal conversion to
finite generated diagrams proves the gcd certificate bound with two extra
remainder facts and the bound for positive and negative coprimeness instances.
The reduction itself is also checked: on coprime inputs, the least number of
remainder facts in a certificate for $\gcd(a,b)=1$ equals $D(a,b)$
(\lean{ShortGCD.callsRem_eq_D}), and $G$ agrees with $D$
(\lean{ShortGCD.G_eq_D}).

The Lean definitions from certificates are
\lean{ShortGCD.callsCoprimeness} for the book's relation
$\perp\!\!\!\perp$ in $\mathcal N_\varepsilon$, with positive inputs
(\lean{ShortGCD.BookCoprime}), and \lean{ShortGCD.callsGcd} and
\lean{ShortGCD.callsRemGcd} for the gcd in $\mathcal N_0$ and
$\mathcal N_\varepsilon$. Adding the facts $\eq_0(a)=\mathrm{ff}$ and
$\eq_0(b)=\mathrm{ff}$ is checked to turn a certificate for $\gcd(a,b)=1$ into
one for $\perp\!\!\!\perp$ (\lean{ShortGCD.certifiesCoprimeness_of_certifiesCoprime}),
so on positive coprime pairs the complexity agrees with the reduction
(\lean{ShortGCD.callsCoprimeness_eq_callsRem}).

The theorems
\lean{ShortGCD.not_conjecture_3_4},
\lean{ShortGCD.not_main_conjecture_4F5} and
\lean{ShortGCD.not_main_conjecture_4F5_Neps} state that no $r>0$ satisfies
$\calls_{\rem}(\mathcal N_\varepsilon,\perp\!\!\!\perp,a,b)\geq r\log_2 a$
for infinitely many $a\geq b\geq1$, and that no $K>0$ satisfies
$2\log_2\max(x,y)\leq K\calls_{\rem}(\mathcal A,\gcd,x,y)$ for infinitely
many pairs in $\mathbb N^2$, for either structure. The original
$O(\log a/\log\log a)$ bounds combine
\lean{ShortGCD.callsCoprimeness_le} and \lean{ShortGCD.callsGcd_le}, which bound
the intrinsic complexities by $G(a,b)+2$, with \lean{ShortGCD.uniform_real_bound};
\lean{ShortGCD.eventually_calls_le} states the resulting $o(\log)$ bound. The
general theory of uniform
processes, Theorem~4F.1 and the lower bounds of Section~1 are cited, not
formalized. Theorem~4F.1 is needed only to restate these intrinsic bounds in
terms of a single best uniform process, not for the refutations themselves.

Theorem~\ref{thm:programs} is checked in the eight modules
\lean{RegisterProgram} through \lean{RegisterSmallStep}. Under
\lean{ShortGCD.RegisterProgram}, the theorem \lean{maxCost_step} proves the
fixed-parameter recurrence, and \lean{Fixed.limsup_profile_le} proves its
coefficient $3/p$. The syntax development gives finite body sets for the four
internal symbols above. \lean{Syntax.run_invokes} translates generator runs
into evaluation trees with exactly the same remainder count;
\lean{Syntax.accepts_eval} includes final verification.
\lean{Syntax.eval_head_correct} proves correctness of every terminating
evaluation, and \lean{Syntax.eval_head_exists} proves existence on every
input. The quantified bound is \lean{Syntax.arbitrarily_small_program};
\lean{Syntax.eventually_eval_bound} gives every coefficient greater than
$3/p$ for a fixed $p$. \lean{Syntax.Machine.eval_iff_steps} proves the
correspondence with the specialized small-step machine in both directions.
\lean{Syntax.Machine.head_correct} and \lean{Syntax.Machine.head_exists}
give correctness and convergence, while
\lean{Syntax.Machine.arbitrarily_small_program} states the bound directly

Proposition~\ref{prop:loglog} is checked in the module \lean{ShortGCD.AlmostAll}.
\lean{ShortGCD.AlmostAll.card_within_le} bounds the number of pairs
$2\leq b<a\leq N$ with a derivation of $1$ of length at most $d$ by

\subsection{Integer parameters used in Lean}
To avoid real rounding in the construction itself, the formal proof uses, for
$j=\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor\geq32$,
\[
p=\lfloor j/4\rfloor,\quad k=2^p,\quad h=2^{2p}+1,\quad
J=\lfloor n/p\rfloor+1,\quad
\ell_1=\lceil3n/4\rceil,\quad \ell_2=\lceil n/3\rceil.
\]
Write $\widehat T(X)=\max_{a,b\leq X}G(a,b)$. The finite construction proves,
for arbitrary natural parameters satisfying $k\geq1$, $k^2<h$, $Y\geq1$,
$2X\leq ZY$, and $X\leq Zk^J$,
\[
\widehat T(X)\leq\max\{h+1+\widehat T(Y),\ h+3J+2+2\widehat T(Z)\}.
\]
Taking $X=2^n$, $Y=2^{\ell_1}$, $Z=2^{\ell_2}$ gives the required hypotheses.
Lean checks
\[
(h+1)j\leq64n,\quad (h+3J+2)j\leq64n,\quad
j-2\leq\log_2^{\mathrm{nat}}\ell_1,\log_2^{\mathrm{nat}}\ell_2,
\quad 8\ell_1\leq7n,\quad16\ell_2\leq7n.
\]
The last estimates and $8j\leq9(j-2)$ give a factor $63/64$ for either
recursive contribution after clearing denominators. Taking $C\geq4096$ and
large enough for the finite initial segment proves
$\widehat T(2^n)\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor\leq Cn$ for all $n$.
Comparing integer floors and ceilings with real base-two logarithms yields the
stated real bound. These parameter choices change only absolute constants,
not the proof mechanism or its asymptotic order. The refinement in
\lean{ShortGCD.SharpBound} uses the tunable parameters of
Corollary~\ref{cor:six}, with recursive size bounds
$2^{\lceil\alpha n\rceil}$ and $2^{\lceil\beta n\rceil}$. It applies the same
finite recurrence, uses the original bound to justify a finite upper limit,
and checks the rounding limits needed to preserve the coefficient $6$.
